## 1. Reloading Is Inherently Hazardous
The assembly and handloading of ammunition involves flammable and explosive propellants, high-energy primers, and extreme chamber pressures that can exceed 65,000 psi (4,480 bar). Improperly assembled ammunition can cause **catastrophic firearm destruction, severe personal injury, permanent disability, or death**.

## 2. Theoretical Mathematical Models
All interior ballistics calculations in LoadBench—including peak pressure, muzzle velocity, burn rate progression, and temperature coefficients—are derived from mathematical approximations (Noble-Abel equation of state, Vieille's burning law, and empirical closed-bomb data). Physical firearms deviate from theoretical models due to:
- **Component Lot Variations**: Propellant burn rates ($Ba$) vary by $\pm 3-5\%$ between production batches.
- **Primer Brisance Deviations**: Different primer brands, lots, and cup hardnesses generate widely varying initial pressures.
- **Case Capacity Discrepancies**: Internal water capacity varies substantially across brass manufacturers and individual lot wall thicknesses.
- **Firearm Chamber & Bore Dimensional Tolerances**: Custom match chambers, tight necks, throat erosion, barrel groove dimensions, and land engraving angles drastically alter peak pressure.

## 3. Mandatory 10% Reduction Rule
**ALWAYS REDUCE STARTING CHARGE BY A MINIMUM OF 10% BELOW ANY MAXIMUM CHARGE ESTIMATE AND WORK UP IN SLOW, CONTROLLED INCREMENTS (0.2 TO 0.5 GRAINS).**

Immediately cease firing and inspect fired cases upon observing any indication of excessive pressure:
- Flattened, cratered, or pierced primers
- Difficult or sticky bolt lift
- Shiny ejector marks or brass extrusion into the bolt face ejector hole
- Head separation rings or case split necks
